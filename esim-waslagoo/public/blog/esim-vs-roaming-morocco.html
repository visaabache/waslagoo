eSIM vs Roaming in Morocco: What UK & EU Travellers Actually Pay

Morocco sits outside the EU and outside the UK's standard roaming zones, so the "it'll just work like at home" assumption doesn't apply here. Here's what your existing SIM actually costs in Morocco, compared to an eSIM bought in advance.

Why Morocco catches people out

Most UK and EU travellers are used to one of two things: EU "roam-like-home" rules, or a UK provider's included-Europe allowance. Morocco qualifies for neither. It's treated as a separate, often more expensive roaming zone by nearly every major UK and EU network, which means the free or cheap data you're used to elsewhere in Europe doesn't carry over.

The result is that a lot of travellers only discover the actual cost once they're already there and checking Instagram at the airport — or worse, after they land back home and see the bill.

What UK networks actually charge

Rates and bundles change, and your exact plan matters, but here's the general shape of it for 2026:

Rates vary by specific plan and change over time — always check your provider's current Morocco/international roaming page before you travel. Figures above reflect typical/standard rates without a destination-specific add-on unless noted.

The pattern to notice: where a network sells a dedicated Morocco or "Zone D" travel pack (like EE's daily pass or Orange's Holiday Morocco bundle), the cost is often reasonable. Where you rely on the standard pay-as-you-go rate with no add-on — which is what happens if you simply forget to set one up — the cost can be dramatically higher, sometimes by 10–50x.

What an eSIM costs instead

A Morocco-specific eSIM is a fixed, known price you pay once, before you fly — no daily charges, no per-MB surprises, and no risk of forgetting to activate anything at the airport. As a reference point, a 10GB / 15-day Morocco eSIM plan currently runs about €29.50 (roughly £25) — enough for maps, messaging apps, social media and browsing across a two-week trip.

The trade-off is worth being honest about: an eSIM is a separate data-only line, so calls and texts on your actual phone number still route through your home network as normal (and may still incur their own charges) — but data, which is what actually drains a bill fastest, is fully covered and fully predictable.

Which one actually makes sense for your trip

If your network happens to sell a proper Morocco travel pack and you're confident you'll remember to activate it before landing, that can be a perfectly reasonable option — Orange's is a good example. But for most UK travellers specifically, where the standard non-bundle rate is what kicks in by default, an eSIM removes the guesswork entirely: one price, set up from home, working the moment you land.
