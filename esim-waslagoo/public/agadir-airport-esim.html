Agadir Airport eSIM: get online the moment you land

Roughly four hours in the air from the UK, and the last thing you want is a queue, a sales pitch and a roaming bill before you have found your bag. This guide shows how UK travellers get online at Agadir Al Massira, and how to choose the right amount of data in about a minute.

The short answer

For most UK holidaymakers the simplest route is to install a Morocco eSIM at home on Wi‑Fi and switch it on when you land. You're online at passport control, your UK SIM stays in place for calls and texts, and you know the price before you fly.

Orange, inwi and Maroc Telecom all run SIM kiosks in the arrivals area, which is a sound back‑up if you want a Moroccan phone number. But opening hours, queues and sales tactics vary, and leaving your UK line to roam is usually the costliest option.

Your first hour at Agadir Airport, minute by minute

Agadir Al Massira has a single terminal, so there is not much to navigate. What matters is what your phone is doing while you move through it. Here is the sequence that works.

  1. The day before · at home

    Install your eSIM on Wi‑Fi

    Buy a Morocco plan, scan the QR code and let your phone add the line. It takes a couple of minutes and needs an internet connection, so do it before you leave the UK, not at the departure gate. Rename the line "Morocco" so you can spot it later.

    On your phone: Settings → Mobile data (or SIM / Connections) → Add eSIM. The wording differs by phone.

  2. On the plane

    Get your lines ready

    Keep flight mode on for the flight. Check that data roaming is off for your UK line and set to use the Morocco line for mobile data, so your phone never reaches for the expensive connection by mistake. Many travel plans only start their validity clock once they first connect in Morocco, but check the plan you buy.

  3. Wheels down · T+0

    Switch flight mode off

    Your phone will look for a Moroccan network, which will be Orange, inwi or Maroc Telecom depending on your plan. Give it a minute. Once you see signal bars you are online, and no kiosk was needed.

  4. Passport control · T+10 to T+45 min

    Use the queue well

    Immigration queues vary with how many flights have landed together, and many UK services arrive in the evening. Use the wait to message your driver or hotel with your ETA and check your onward plans. Have your accommodation name and address ready, ideally screenshotted in case you are ever offline.

  5. Baggage reclaim · T+30 to T+60 min

    Cash, and a plan B

    Cash machines, currency desks and the SIM kiosks sit in the arrivals area. Airport currency desks can give slightly worse rates than exchange offices in town, so change only what you need for the transfer. If you want a Moroccan number, the kiosks are here too.

  6. Taxi rank or pre-booked driver · T+45 to T+75 min

    Check before you go

    Grand taxis at the rank generally use fixed fares by destination and want cash in dirhams. Agree the price before your bags go in the boot. Open your maps app and watch the route: it is roughly a 30–40 minute drive to central Agadir and closer to an hour to Taghazout.

  7. Hotel or riad · T+90 min

    Save your data for outdoors

    Use the hotel Wi‑Fi for big downloads and uploads, and keep your eSIM for maps, messages and getting around.

Flying to Agadir from the UK

Direct flights to Agadir currently operate from a long list of UK airports, including London Gatwick, Stansted and Luton, Manchester, Birmingham, Bristol, Leeds Bradford, Edinburgh and Glasgow, with some routes running seasonally. easyJet, Jet2, Ryanair, TUI and British Airways are among the airlines. Flight time is typically around 3 hours 45 minutes to 4 hours. Schedules change, so check your airline for your exact dates.

Seven tips specifically for UK travellers

  1. Keep your UK SIM for texts. Banks and apps often send one-time codes by SMS. Switch off data roaming on that line so it never carries data by accident, and check with your provider about any charges for receiving texts abroad.
  2. Check your phone supports eSIM and is unlocked. Dial *#06#: if an EID number appears, the phone supports eSIM. If it is network-locked, ask your provider to unlock it; UK providers are required to on request.
  3. Set your mobile data line manually. Don't leave it on "automatic". Pick the Morocco line for data.
  4. Download offline maps before you fly. Save your hotel, the route from the airport and the area you are staying in.
  5. Don't plan around ride-hailing apps. Reports suggest Uber and Bolt-style apps are not reliably available in Agadir, so check before you fly and carry some cash for taxis.
  6. Stop apps updating on mobile data. Set app updates, cloud photo backups and podcast downloads to Wi‑Fi only.
  7. Expect busy arrivals in school holidays. Half-term, Easter and summer flights land in waves, which is another reason to be online before the kiosk queue forms.

Frequently asked questions

Does Agadir airport sell SIM cards?

Yes. Orange, inwi and Maroc Telecom operate SIM kiosks in the arrivals area, generally after passport control near baggage reclaim, and you will need your passport to register. Opening hours vary, so don't count on buying one after a very late landing.

Is an eSIM better than an airport SIM card?

For most short UK trips, yes: it is ready before you land and avoids the kiosk queue. A physical SIM is better if you need a Moroccan phone number or you are staying a long time and using a lot of data in remote areas.

Will an eSIM work with my UK phone?

Most iPhones from the XS and XR (2018) onwards and many recent Samsung Galaxy and Google Pixel models support eSIM, and the phone must be network-unlocked. Dial *#06#: if an EID number appears, your phone supports eSIM.

When should I install my eSIM?

Before you leave home, on Wi‑Fi. Many travel plans only start their validity when they first connect to a network in Morocco, but check the details of the plan you buy.

How much data do I need for a week in Agadir?

As a rule of thumb, light users (maps and messages) often get by on 2 to 3 GB, typical holidaymakers sharing photos and using social media tend to need around 5 GB, and heavy users on video calls or sharing a hotspot may want 10 GB or an unlimited plan. The planner on this page turns that into a plan suggestion.

Can I use my phone as a hotspot with an eSIM?

Often yes, but it depends on the plan's terms, so check before you buy. Sharing data with a family uses it faster, and the planner has a hotspot option for that.

What if I land late at night?

Many flights from the UK reach Agadir in the evening or after dark. Kiosk hours vary, which is another reason to have your eSIM installed before you fly. Pre-book a transfer or make sure you have dirhams for the taxi rank.

Do I need cash at Agadir airport?

Yes. Grand taxis at the airport generally take cash in dirhams. There are cash machines and currency desks in arrivals; desks can offer slightly worse rates than exchange offices in town, so change only what you need for the transfer.

Will my UK phone plan work in Morocco?

Morocco is outside the EU, and UK inclusive-roaming allowances usually don't cover it. Some networks sell a daily pass and others charge steep per-MB rates, so check your network's Morocco tariff. Our roaming comparison explains what to look for.
